Level calibration: not set (optional) Details & setup
Transducer

The transducer choice sets the dB HL to dB SPL conversion. Earbud values were measured for wired Apple EarPods and are an approximation for AirPods. For earbuds, turn off noise cancellation, transparency, Conversation Awareness, Headphone Accommodations and volume limits during calibration and screening.

Optional. Calibrate once per sitting: the result is kept on this device for 4 hours and reused for later screenings with the same device, transducer and volume setting. Changing the transducer requires a new calibration. Do not change the device volume after calibrating. Without calibration, tone levels rely on an unverified assumption about the device output.

Option 1: physical SPL calibration (continuous 80 dB SPL reference)

Place one earcup (HD 280 Pro: flat-plate adapter) or earbud on a coupler matching the transducer's RETSPL reference and select Play coupler tone (a steady 1000 Hz tone in both sides). Adjust the device volume until the meter reads 80 dB SPL, check the other side, then select Save. The output level is only nominal until verified with the meter.

Option 2: normal-hearing reference listener (no meter)

A person with normal hearing should wear the headphones in a quiet room and calibrate once for everyone screened in this sitting. Screened participants do not repeat this procedure. First set the device volume so the steady 1000 Hz tone has roughly Medium loudness. Then make four on-screen settings: the tone starts either too soft or too loud, and you select Louder or Softer until its loudness is Medium, neither soft nor loud, then select Medium. This is a loudness judgment, not a most-comfortable-level judgment. The same tone is presented to both ears (diotically). The mean setting is assumed to be 75 dB SPL per ear: the 80 dB SPL monaural Medium anchor minus a provisional 5 dB binaural loudness-summation correction (Schlittenlacher et al., 2014). actual output can differ substantially between reference listeners. Use physical SPL calibration when possible. The starting level changes each time, so do not count button presses. Cancel if the tone is uncomfortable.

Room noise: off (optional) Details & setup

This is a rough estimate, not a calibrated sound level measurement or an OSHA compliance check. Your normal speaking voice is assumed to be 62.5 dBA (within 60-65 dBA). Keep the microphone in the same position after setting the reference, and use headphones during the hearing screening.

Before starting the hearing screener, read aloud at normal conversational volume for 8 seconds: "I am checking the sound around me so that I can use my hearing screener in a quiet place." Repeat if needed. Afterward, stop speaking.

Green means all four estimated octave-band levels are at or below their reference limits; red means at least one exceeds its limit. Grey means monitoring is off or no usable estimate is available. Limits are 47, 44, 54, and 59 dB SPL at 500, 1000, 2000, and 4000 Hz (OSHA-based limits adjusted for Sennheiser HD 280 Pro headphones). Earbuds without noise cancellation block much less room noise, so these limits are too lenient for them.
